Strobe: drone confirmation by propeller hum

Strobe confirms drones by their propeller hum. A normal 30 fps camera proposes objects. Inside each box, the event camera stream is checked for a blade-pass frequency peak at hundreds of Hz, which a 30 fps camera cannot see. An agent decides what to look at next: re-check a box with a longer or tighter event window, track it, raise an alert, or ask a human. Everything runs in OpenCV 5 on AWS Graviton.

Slide 1: the 30 fps RGB video of FRED sequence 210 with the annotated drone box and a zoomed view where no rotor is visible, next to a bar strip of brighter-minus-darker event counts inside the drone box, one bar per 0.5 ms, with ticks at the measured blade-pass period.

Slide 2: an orbitable x-y-time point cloud of the events in 40 ms on the drone. Black points: a pixel got brighter. Grey points: darker. The two alternate in bands as the blades pass.

Slide 3: the event spectrum of a box in the drone clip next to a box in a clip with no drone, computed live on the server by strobe.freq.box_spectrum (OpenCV 5 calcHist and dft) for each click. The shaded band is the propeller band, 150 to 1000 Hz. Power is shown relative to the band median on a log scale. A box hums when its band peak stands far above the local spectral floor and it holds enough events.

Slide 4, the math. (1) Sampling: a 30 fps camera only sees frequencies up to its Nyquist limit of 15 Hz; a propeller blade-pass rate of several hundred Hz folds (aliases) to a slow apparent rate, the wagon-wheel effect. Events binned at 0.1 ms (10 kHz) see up to 5 kHz. (2) Fourier transform: the brighter-minus-darker event count inside the box over time, multiplied by a Hanning window, goes through cv.dft; the power spectrum shows the blade-pass peak. X(f) = sum over t of x(t) w(t) exp(-i 2 pi f t). (3) Score: the peak power divided by the median power within plus or minus 100 Hz of it. Thresholds from lane L1, chosen on FRED training data: ambiguous above 7.9, hum above 98. (4) Frequency resolution is 1/T for a window of T seconds, so a longer window sharpens the peak; this is why the agent asks for a longer window (request_window) when a spectrum is ambiguous. (5) OpenCV 5 calls in order: calcHist (event binning), dft (spectrum), connectedComponentsWithStats (event blobs and masks), dnn (RGB detector).

Slide 5: the agent's recorded steps from lane L4's evaluation traces: perception tools (detect_frame, box_spectrum, request_window, frequency_map, track), the human approval request, the alert, and the finish. The Approve and Reject buttons record a decision on the server only; nothing external is triggered. The approval inside the recorded run came from an automatic stand-in for the operator, marked SIMULATED in the trace.

Slide 6: false alarms with RGB only versus RGB plus events (pending until the evaluation file exists), and the OpenCV 5 benchmark on AWS Graviton4 c8g.xlarge versus x86 c7i.xlarge, p50 milliseconds at 4 threads, from bench/results.
