    Frequency Vision: drone confirmation by propeller hum

    Frequency Vision confirms drones by their propeller hum. A normal 30 fps camera proposes objects. Inside each box, the event camera stream is checked for a blade-pass frequency peak at hundreds of Hz, which a 30 fps camera cannot see. An agent decides what to look at next: re-check a box with a longer or tighter event window, track it, raise an alert, or ask a human. Everything runs in OpenCV 5 on AWS Graviton.

    Logo: a quadcopter with spinning propellers next to the name Frequency Vision, and a counter that shows the selected clip's measured propeller frequency in Hz.

    Slide 1: the 30 fps RGB video, played at half speed, with the annotated drone box and a zoomed view where no rotor is visible, next to a bar strip of brighter-minus-darker event counts inside the drone box, one bar per 0.5 ms over 40 ms, with orange ticks at the measured blade-pass period.

    Slide 2: an orbitable x-y-time point cloud of the events in 40 ms on the drone, with x, y and time axes labelled. Black points: a pixel got brighter (ON event). Grey points: darker (OFF event). The orange lines are one per measured blade pass. The "vs empty" view stacks a same-size patch from the no-drone clip (FRED sequence 138) under it. The strip below is the same brighter-minus-darker count per 0.5 ms.

    Slide 3: the event spectrum of a box in the drone clip next to a box in a clip with no drone, computed live on the server by box_spectrum (OpenCV 5 calcHist and dft) for each click. The shaded band is the propeller band, 150 to 1000 Hz. Power is shown relative to the band median on a log scale. A box hums when its band peak stands at least 98 times above the local spectral floor and it holds at least 200 events. Window buttons set the event window length; the default, 100 ms, scored best on the FRED test split (area under the curve 0.758 against the busiest no-drone patch, 0.753 at 50 ms, 0.702 at 20 ms). Thumbnails pick the frame and box.

    Slide 4, the math, in four panels (tabs on narrow screens). (1) Sampling: a 30 fps camera only sees frequencies up to its Nyquist limit of 15 Hz; a propeller blade-pass rate of several hundred Hz folds (aliases) to a slow apparent rate, the wagon-wheel effect: f_alias = |f - k*30|. Events binned at 0.1 ms (10 kHz) see up to 5 kHz. (2) Fourier transform: the brighter-minus-darker event count inside the box over time, multiplied by a Hanning window, goes through cv.dft; the power spectrum shows the blade-pass peak. X(f) = sum over t of x(t) w(t) exp(-i 2 pi f t). (3) Score: the peak power divided by the median power within plus or minus 100 Hz of it. Thresholds chosen on FRED training data: ambiguous above 7.9, hum above 98. (4) Frequency resolution is 1/T for a window of T seconds, so a longer window sharpens the peak; this is why the agent asks for a longer window (request_window) when a spectrum is ambiguous. (5) OpenCV 5 calls in order: calcHist (event binning), dft (spectrum), connectedComponentsWithStats (event blobs and masks), dnn (RGB detector).

    Slide 5: the agent's recorded steps from the evaluation traces (eval_v3), grouped into three rows: look, listen and decide. Play shows one step about every 0.6 seconds and stops at the approval step until you press Approve or Reject; the default episode is the drone that appears late (FRED 142), where the camera detector finds nothing and the frequency map finds the drone. Each step is a card: look (detect_frame, track), listen (box_spectrum, request_window, frequency_map) with the measured peak in Hz, coloured orange when it passes the hum test, outlined when ambiguous, grey when there is no hum; decide (ask_human, raise_alert, finish). Episodes: a drone (FRED 210), a drone that appears late (FRED 142), no drone (FRED 138), each planned by Claude Sonnet 4.6 on Amazon Bedrock, and a fixed-rule baseline on FRED 210. Nothing plays until you press Play. "Run agent live" starts the agent on the server on the clip picked above (DJI Mini 2 is FRED sequence 202), planned by Claude Sonnet 4.6 on Amazon Bedrock (the fixed-rule planner takes over after 100 Bedrock runs in a UTC day or on a Bedrock error, and the page names the planner that ran). It calls the real tools (detector, box spectrum, tracker), stops before the critical alert and waits up to 60 seconds for your Approve or Reject. With no answer the run ends unconfirmed, waiting for an operator, and no alert is raised. At the end the page shows the clip's expected frequency and how much the agent's box overlaps the FRED ground-truth box. One live run per visitor and at most three at once. The Approve and Reject buttons record a decision on the server only; nothing external is triggered. The approval inside the recorded run came from an automatic stand-in for the operator, marked SIMULATED in the trace.
